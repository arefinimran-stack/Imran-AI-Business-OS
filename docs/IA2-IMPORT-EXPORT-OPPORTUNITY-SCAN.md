# IA² Import / Export Opportunity Scan — Bangladesh, Batch 01

**Research date:** 15 November 2026  
**Data window:** FY2026–27 July–October exports and July–September imports where available.  
**Purpose:** Prioritise product-market pairs for buyer discovery and trade facilitation. This is a screening scan, not a forecast or recommendation to buy inventory.

## 1. Export signals — Bangladesh

EPB data reported by BSS show total exports of USD 17.09 billion in July–October FY2026–27, up 5.88% year-on-year. Non-traditional sectors grew faster:
- Chemical products: +27%.
- Jute and jute goods: +24.88%.
- Specialised textiles: +16.52%.
- Leather and leather products: +12.47%.
- Engineering products: +12.44%.

Source: BSS, 4 November 2026, reporting EPB data: https://www.bssnews.net/news/441848

RMG exports reached USD 10.58 billion in July–September FY2026–27, up 6.10%. Knitwear was USD 5.96 billion (+6.88%) and woven garments USD 4.61 billion (+5.11%). Source: BSS / EPB: https://www.bssnews.net/business/433319

## 2. Import signals — Bangladesh

Bangladesh Bank's commodity-wise import statistics, compiled from NBR customs data, show July–September FY2026–27 imports of USD 20.43 billion, up 15.4% year-on-year. Relevant categories include:
- RMG-related goods: USD 4.61 billion (+2.7%).
- Textile and articles thereof: USD 2.35 billion (+8.3%).
- Yarn: USD 987.5 million (+9.7%).
- Raw cotton: USD 727.5 million (-14.4%).
- Staple fibre: USD 332.2 million (-5.7%).
- Chemicals: USD 1.01 billion (+4.9%).
- Plastics and rubber articles: USD 938.3 million (+9.6%).
- Pharmaceutical products: USD 167.7 million (+47.5%).

Official source: Bangladesh Bank, commodity-wise import statistics: https://www.bb.org.bd/en/index.php/econdata/bop_imp_statistics

Potential sourcing countries are hypotheses for buyer/supplier discovery, not confirmed cheapest or approved sources.

## 3. Commercial interpretation

- Strong import value is not the same as attractive margin. It may reflect high input dependence, price inflation or commodity price movement.
- A falling import category can still represent a large addressable market; raw cotton remains a significant input even though the reported quarter declined.
- Import agency requires clear buyer mandates, product specification, supplier due diligence, landed-cost calculation, payment-risk review and import-policy compliance.
- Export brokerage requires a real overseas buyer requirement, a verified exporter/factory, sample and quality acceptance, Incoterms, payment security and shipping documentation.
- Do not hold inventory or finance trade flows without committed buyers, supplier contracts, cash-flow and foreign-exchange risk analysis.

## 4. IA² first commercial focus

1. **Japan apparel buyer desk** — highest-priority near-term lead generation based on public buyer discussions and Bangladesh market signals.
2. **Bangladesh market-entry facilitation** — paid legal/corporate setup and local operating support for foreign businesses.
3. **Textile-input sourcing intelligence** — connect verified overseas mills and chemical/fibre suppliers with local importers and manufacturers.
4. **Non-RMG export buyer discovery** — jute, specialised textiles, leather and engineering products.
5. **Pharma / chemical trade** — research only until regulatory requirements, licensed importers and product registrations are confirmed.

## 5. Mandatory next research layer

For every product-market pair, collect:
- HS code at 6–8 digits and official tariff description.
- Monthly and annual value, quantity and unit price.
- Import/export partner countries and concentration.
- Named importers/exporters from lawful public or licensed trade data.
- Current buyer RFQs, supplier capability and certifications.
- Duties, VAT, supplementary duty, trade remedies and rules of origin.
- Import Policy Order 2026–2029 requirements and amendments.
- LC / payment terms, freight, insurance, port and lead-time costs.
- Working-capital requirement and gross margin under downside scenarios.

The next data pass should use Bangladesh Bank, NBR, EPB, ITC Trade Map / UN Comtrade and verified buyer / supplier disclosures. Use paid shipment databases only if access and cost are justified.

## 6. Decision rule

Do not recommend a product for trading until there is at least one verified buyer, one qualified supplier, a complete landed-cost model, regulatory clearance, payment-risk assessment and a realistic margin after financing and logistics.
