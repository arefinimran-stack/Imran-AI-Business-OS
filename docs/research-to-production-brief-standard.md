# IMRAN AI — Research-to-Production Brief Standard

Version 1.0 | Bangladesh-first | Asia/Dhaka | Operating editorial specification

## 1. Purpose

Convert market and audience research into production-ready editorial packages across Arefin Legal & Corporate, Imran Arefin, Arefin Avenue, IA² Holdings, C3 Lifestyle and Bold Cotton. The central desk coordinates evidence, production and quality control without flattening the six brand identities.

A completed brief must let a writer, designer, editor or presenter produce the asset without guessing facts, inventing personal experience or changing the approved angle. Approval for production is not approval to publish.

## 2. Required brief record

Every brief must record:
- Stable brief ID, version and revision history.
- Primary division/brand key and working title.
- Specific audience segment, audience question and objective.
- Research question and editorial angle.
- Evidence status: not_started, researching, evidence_checked, needs_review, approved_for_production.
- Claim register: exact claim, source, date, URL, exact supporting passage/table/section, confidence and limitation.
- Genre, format, key takeaway and script/article/carousel outline.
- Visual direction: palette, composition, footage/photo needs, typography, rights and consent.
- Platform adaptations: native title, caption, dimensions, duration, subtitles, thumbnail and CTA suitability.
- Legal, professional, privacy, safety, copyright, consent, reputational and product-claim risks.
- Named reviewer, approval state and date.
- Post-publication metrics, observation window and next experiment.

## 3. Evidence and claim control

### Evidence hierarchy
1. Primary: legislation, judgments, gazettes, official notices, regulator/government datasets, company filings, supplier specifications, test reports, first-party analytics and direct interviews with consent.
2. High-quality secondary: peer-reviewed research, recognised multilateral institutions, established industry bodies and named-source trade journalism.
3. Contextual: competitor posts, creator commentary, forums and social discussion. Qualitative signals only, not proof of market size or universal behaviour.
4. Discovery only: search snippets, AI summaries, unattributed charts and viral posts. Never use as final evidence.

### Claim register
For every material factual statement, record claim text, source title and issuing body/author, publication and access dates, direct URL, exact section/table/paragraph, proposition supported, evidence type, confidence, caveats and reviewer. Split compound claims when one source supports only part of a sentence.

Do not turn correlation into causation, a sample into a national conclusion, advertising reach into active users, or export-market growth into proof of local retail demand. If a source is inaccessible, stale, ambiguous or contradictory, state that and downgrade confidence. Never fill gaps with plausible-sounding facts.

### Evidence gates
- not_started: no research performed.
- researching: sources being gathered; no factual publication claims.
- evidence_checked: every material claim checked against its source.
- needs_review: specialist, legal, technical, product or privacy review outstanding.
- approved_for_production: evidence and scope approved for production; not publication approval.

## 4. Production structures

### Short vertical video (20–60 seconds)
Opening question/tension; context; two or three useful points with on-screen support; qualification or limitation; educational close. Deliver the promised value early.

### Carousel/document (5–9 panels)
Audience question; scope/definition; key points; practical checklist or example; caveat/source note; summary. Keep each panel to one idea and make source notes readable.

### Article/newsletter
Headline and standfirst; question and scope; evidence-led sections; competing considerations; practical implications; limitations; dated sources.

### Documentary/interview
Editorial premise; verified subject and location details; consent/release status; question list; shot list; fact-check of names, captions and chronology. Never present a staged or generated scene as documentary fact.

### Product demonstration/review
Verified product/sample identity; specification source and test method; test conditions; observed facts versus claims; limitations and commercial disclosure. No invented performance, certification, durability or health claims.

## 5. Platform adaptation

- Facebook: community-readable framing, accessible captions and useful discussion prompts; legal content must not solicit work.
- Instagram: strong visual opening, concise Reels or saveable carousels, readable subtitles and mobile-first text.
- YouTube Shorts: one complete idea, accurate title and early delivery of promised value.
- YouTube long-form: deeper context, chapter structure, source references and accurate thumbnail/title.
- LinkedIn: original professional insight, evidence and practical frameworks; no generic engagement bait.

Adapt packaging, not facts. Maintain a canonical source asset and link every derivative to it.

## 6. Division-specific research and production priorities

### A. Arefin Legal & Corporate
Editorial role: public legal literacy and corporate-law education, not client acquisition.
Research lanes: current statutes and amendments; reported judgments and subsequent treatment; court procedure and official practice directions; regulator circulars; company, tax and governance obligations.
Pilot briefs:
1. How to read an official judgment: court, date, issue, holding, ratio and subsequent treatment.
2. Records a small company should organise before a governance or compliance review.
Evidence: official law/gazette, judgment or regulator source; exact citation; current-status check. A secondary explainer alone is insufficient for a legal proposition.
Review: advocate review mandatory. No solicitation, guarantees, superiority claims, individualised advice, confidential matter details or misleading case outcomes. Check current Bangladesh Bar Council requirements.

### B. Imran Arefin — personal professional profile
Editorial role: authentic founder/advocate thought leadership.
Research lanes: shareable practice insights without client details; leadership and operating principles; reading notes; public-interest commentary; learning and discipline.
Pilot briefs:
1. A founder's weekly review: decisions, priorities, lessons and next commitments, using only real supplied or confirmed details.
2. How an advocate separates facts, law, inference and uncertainty when analysing a difficult issue.
Evidence: first-person experience must be supplied or explicitly confirmed. Never invent anecdotes, achievements, clients, credentials, quotations or personal opinions.
Identity: author is Imran Arefin. Portraits must preserve the founder's original reference face. Use only founder-verified contact details in specifically approved placements.

### C. Arefin Avenue
Editorial role: Bangladesh-focused travel, culture, people and place storytelling.
Research lanes: destination access and seasonality; transport and accommodation facts; safety and visitor rules; heritage records; local voices and consent; environmental and community impacts.
Pilot briefs:
1. Destination field guide with verified access, transport, costs and responsible-visitor information.
2. Short human story about a local craft, food tradition or place, with interview consent and provenance checks.
Evidence: official destination/transport notices, site authorities, credible local reporting and direct on-location verification. Confirm current hours, access, fees and safety before release. Do not fabricate visits or present generated scenes as documentary footage.

### D. IA² Holdings
Editorial role: business, SME operations, governance and capital-discipline education.
Research lanes: Bangladesh Bank, BBS, SME Foundation, RJSC, NBR, BSEC and other relevant primary sources; governance; financial recordkeeping; SME operating models; sector conditions.
Pilot briefs:
1. Governance checklist for a growing Bangladeshi SME: roles, records, decision rights and reporting.
2. What a financing-readiness file should contain, distinguishing documentation quality from actual credit eligibility.
Evidence: regulator/government material, audited filings, recognised research and transparent methodology. No funding promises, investment-return claims or unsupported market-size assertions.

### E. C3 Lifestyle
Editorial role: lifestyle editorial and consumer discovery; category positioning remains to be validated.
First confirm intended category, target customer, actual products/services, price range and fulfilment model. Then research category-specific competitors, customer questions, safety/quality standards and local demand.
Pilot briefs:
1. Consumer decision guide for one confirmed C3 category, using transparent criteria and no untested product claims.
2. Curated editorial story about a practical everyday choice, only after category and audience are confirmed.
Evidence: actual product/service details, official standards, credible category research, real customer questions and transparent review method. Do not assume cosmetics, wellness, homeware or another category.

### F. Bold Cotton
Editorial role: product-credible apparel and cotton/fabric education.
Research lanes: actual product range; fibre composition; yarn, weave/knit, GSM and finishing where documented; fit and sizing; construction; care; price, returns and fulfilment; local apparel competition.
Pilot briefs:
1. Fabric school: how to read a garment composition label and distinguish fibre content from weave or knit.
2. Garment-detail film showing real seams, stitching, fit and finishing of a confirmed sample.
Evidence: sewn-in labels, supplier documents, test reports and physical sample inspection. Never invent fibre percentages, GSM, origin, certification, shrinkage, durability, sustainability or performance claims.

## 7. Visual direction and founder identity

Use the separate Brand Visual System for palette and composition. Use one dominant colour, one supporting colour, one accent and a neutral. Make charts traceable to sources.

Founder-led and advocate-led imagery must use Imran Arefin's original reference photograph as the facial identity source. Styling, clothing, lighting, crop, setting and art direction may change; the face must remain the founder's actual recognisable face. Never substitute or blend another person's face. If the approved reference image is unavailable, hold the portrait rather than inventing one.

Use the name Imran Arefin and only the verified professional email and phone supplied by the founder when a public contact block is specifically approved. Do not expose contact details in source code, public repository files or internal metadata.

## 8. Editorial release checklist

- Correct division, audience and objective.
- Every material factual claim has a source and exact support.
- Source date, current status and limitations checked.
- Facts, interpretation and hypothesis separated.
- Script/outline matches evidence and chosen genre.
- Visuals are original, accurate, rights-cleared and accessible.
- Founder face and identity preserved where used.
- Names, contact details, captions, subtitles and translations checked.
- Legal/professional, privacy, safety and product-claim reviews complete.
- Platform-specific packaging checked.
- Explicit reviewer approval recorded.
- Publication separately authorised; production approval is not publication permission.

## 9. Operating sequence

Research question → source collection → claim register → evidence review → editorial angle → script/outline → visual brief → platform adaptations → specialist review → editorial approval → separate publication authorisation → performance review.

No item may skip evidence review because it is topical or visually attractive. No approval may be inferred from a draft, scheduled slot or successful build.
