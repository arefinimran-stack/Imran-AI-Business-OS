# IA² Government AI Funding & Public-Problem Pilot Strategy

**Research date:** 15 November 2026  
**Objective:** Identify lawful, realistic routes to initial public funding for an AI solution that solves a documented government problem.  
**Status:** Opportunity research only. No government endorsement, award, application or meeting is claimed.

## 1. Strongest immediate funding route identified

The ICT Division's official ICT Innovation Fund portal describes grants for individuals and organisations developing ICT innovations that improve public services and socio-economic development. The portal says applications are accepted online and lists public services/e-governance and ICT innovation among funding priorities.

The fund's published programme information lists:
- Small grant: up to BDT 5 lakh for proof-of-concept.
- Medium grant: BDT 5,00,001–10 lakh for MVP and market testing.
- Large grant: BDT 10,00,001–20 lakh for scaling a proven solution; registered companies only.
- Strategic institutional grant: up to BDT 25 lakh for exceptional national-impact projects, subject to institutional registration and ministerial approval.

Official portal: https://ims.ictd.gov.bd/?lang=english  
Innovation grant information: https://ims.ictd.gov.bd/grant/innovation

Important: this is a competitive, milestone-based public grant, not equity investment or an unconditional government payment. The official programme information also describes staged releases, progress reporting, mentorship/monitoring, deliverable handover for public use and possible refund obligations if deliverables are not completed. Read the complete policy and obtain legal/accounting review before applying.

## 2. Current policy and institutional context

- The ICT Division's National AI Policy page says a 16-member committee was formed on 2 November 2026 and the draft policy is due by 15 December 2026. The policy is still being formulated; do not describe a draft as adopted law. https://aipolicy.gov.bd/en
- UNESCO's Bangladesh AI Readiness Assessment identifies governance, data, infrastructure, skills and institutional capacity as areas requiring attention. https://www.unesco.org/en/articles/bangladesh-artificial-intelligence-readiness-assessment-report
- Bangladesh Public Procurement Rules 2025 are effective from 28 September 2025. BPPA says e-GP is mandatory for public procurement and highlights transparency, accountability, competition, value for money and ethics. https://bppa.gov.bd/

## 3. Specific government problem signal: procurement integrity and e-GP

A BPPA international REOI published on 15 October 2026 for e-GP 2.0 business-process re-engineering expressly included:
- AI and emerging-technology use cases.
- Detection of non-compliance with public procurement rules.
- Bid-collusion and bid-rotation red flags.
- Interoperability with external IT systems and procurement automation.

This is unusually direct evidence of a government-defined AI problem. However, that particular EOI closed on 4 November 2026 and is no longer an open opportunity. Its prime-consultant eligibility required at least five years in business, three years of audited financial statements and completed public-sector BPR experience of at least USD 2 million. IA² should not present itself as eligible to lead that assignment. A qualified incumbent could potentially be approached for a lawful JV/sub-consultancy role in a future procurement, subject to the tender rules and conflicts.

Official REOI: https://bppa.gov.bd/upload/publication/2026-10-19-04-40-47-Final--REOI---BPR-%28S-04%29.pdf

## 4. Proposed pilot concept

### Working title
**Procurement Integrity & Compliance Intelligence Assistant**

### User problem
Procurement teams and oversight bodies must review complex tender documents, rules, bid submissions and procurement histories. Manual review can be time-consuming; inconsistent documentation and anomalous patterns can be difficult to triage.

### Proposed system
A secure, human-in-the-loop assistant that:
1. Ingests authorised tender notices, procurement rules, bid documents and approved historical records.
2. Extracts structured tender requirements and deadlines.
3. Maps tender clauses to relevant PPR requirements with paragraph-level source references.
4. Flags missing documents, apparent procedural inconsistencies and unusual patterns for human review.
5. Identifies potential bid-rotation/collusion indicators only as explainable risk signals, not findings of wrongdoing.
6. Produces an auditable review report showing source documents, rule references, reasoning, limitations and reviewer decisions.
7. Keeps all award, rejection, debarment and enforcement decisions with authorised human officials.

### Pilot boundaries
- Use only public or formally authorised data.
- Do not ingest confidential bids or personal data without written authority and security approval.
- No automatic bidder scoring, rejection, award or allegation of fraud.
- Explainable, reproducible flags with false-positive testing.
- Role-based access, audit logs, retention rules and secure hosting.
- Independent evaluation before any operational use.

### Measurable pilot outcomes
- Time to review a tender file.
- Percentage of mandatory-document checks completed.
- Citation accuracy for PPR references.
- Reviewer agreement with flags.
- False-positive rate and missed-issue rate on a labelled test set.
- Audit-trail completeness.
- User satisfaction and cost per review.

## 5. Why IA² should not apply as a prime contractor immediately

The BPPA REOI shows the level of experience and audited financial capacity expected for large government digital-transformation contracts. IA² must build a credible record first:
- Registered entity and tax/compliance documents where required.
- Technical partner with proven e-government delivery.
- AI/ML and public-procurement subject-matter team.
- Secure prototype and test evidence.
- Public-sector reference or university/research partner.
- Clear IP, data protection, support and maintenance model.

A first pilot grant or small institutional proof-of-concept is a more realistic starting point than a large procurement bid.

## 6. Recommended first funding application pathway

1. Review the full ICT Innovation Fund policy, current notices, eligibility, scoring and IP/hand-over clauses.
2. Confirm whether the application should be made by the founder as an individual or through a registered entity.
3. Prepare a 5–8 page concept note, problem evidence, prototype scope, budget, workplan, risk register and measurable outcomes.
4. Assemble a small qualified technical team and a public-procurement/legal subject-matter reviewer.
5. Build a demo using synthetic or public procurement data only.
6. Obtain a letter of interest or problem-validation note from an appropriate institution only through a formal, transparent process.
7. Submit through the official portal if eligibility and programme terms are satisfied.
8. Keep the grant accounting, procurement, reporting and deliverables separate from IA² commercial revenue.

## 7. Alternative public-problem themes to research

- Land-record and mutation application completeness / status navigation.
- Public grievance triage and case routing.
- Bangla-language citizen-service information and form guidance.
- Tax and licensing compliance navigation for SMEs.
- Court and legal-aid information access, without replacing judicial or professional decisions.

Each requires an identified sponsoring institution, lawful data access, baseline evidence, a narrow pilot and independent evaluation.

## 8. Immediate decision

Prioritise a small ICT Innovation Fund proof-of-concept application only after confirming eligibility and grant terms. In parallel, develop the procurement-integrity prototype with synthetic/public data and identify an experienced government-technology partner for future procurement opportunities.

Do not represent a grant as approved, a government department as a client, or an informal discussion as an official mandate.
