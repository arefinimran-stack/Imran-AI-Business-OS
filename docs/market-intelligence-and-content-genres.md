# IMRAN AI — Market Intelligence & Content Genre System

**Version:** 1.0  
**Market focus:** Bangladesh first; international reference markets where relevant.  
**Review cadence:** Weekly signal scan; monthly market and competitor synthesis; quarterly positioning review.  
**Status:** Operating editorial framework. Market observations below are hypotheses and research leads unless explicitly attributed to a source.

## 1. Purpose

The content operation must behave like six connected specialist publications, not one account publishing interchangeable posts. Each division needs:
- a defined audience and audience problem;
- its own market, competitor and cultural signal watch;
- a broad but disciplined genre portfolio;
- an evidence standard suited to its subject;
- multiple native formats, not only static graphics;
- a learning loop based on actual account performance and audience feedback.

The central desk shares research methods, production infrastructure and quality control. It does not flatten brand identities.

## 2. Market evidence and limitations

### Bangladesh digital distribution

DataReportal's Digital 2026 Bangladesh report uses data largely from October 2025. It reports 64.0 million social-media user identities, 49.8 million YouTube advertising-reach users, 64.0 million Facebook advertising-reach users and 9.15 million Instagram advertising-reach users. These are platform advertising-planning estimates and social identities, not verified unique active users. Instagram's reported potential advertising reach grew 27.1% between October 2024 and October 2025. Treat all figures with the source's methodology caveats. Source: https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2026-bangladesh

### Online purchase behaviour

The BBS ICT Access and Use by Households and Individuals Survey 2024–25, reported by Bangladesh Sangbad Sangstha on 2 June 2026, found 14.4% of individuals used the internet to buy goods or services; Dhaka division was 21.3%. Among online shoppers, clothing, footwear and sporting goods represented 70.3% of purchases, and cash on delivery was used by 86.8%. This is useful national context, not evidence of demand for any one brand or product. Source: https://www.bssnews.net/special-stories/391786

### Apparel and textiles

DHL's 1 March 2026 overview describes Bangladesh's export apparel industry as shifting toward product diversification and higher-value apparel, with cotton remaining important while man-made fibres and blends gain strategic relevance. Bangladesh Bank's Q4 FY26 RMG review reports export performance and sector conditions, including exchange-rate, production-cost, energy, labour and international trade pressures. These export-market signals should not be confused with local retail demand. Sources:
- https://www.dhl.com/discover/en-bd/e-commerce-advice/e-commerce-sector-guides/understanding-the-rmg-sector-in-bangladesh
- https://www.bb.org.bd/pub/quaterly/rmg/rmg%20april-june%20fy26.pdf

### Travel and tourism

The Business Standard reported rising search interest in solo travel in Bangladesh in October 2026. Bangladesh Monitor's July 2026 coverage identified digital booking, experiential tourism, domestic accommodation digitalisation and trust/transparency as important travel-sector themes. These are editorial leads to investigate, not proof that every destination or travel segment is growing. Sources:
- https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/solace-going-solo-bangladeshs-pursuit-self-discovery-through-travel-1580046
- https://www.bangladeshmonitor.com.bd/en/country-s-travel-sector-undergoing-digital-transformation

### Corporate and SME context

The Business Standard reported in August 2026 that 77% of 11.7 million CMSME entrepreneurs were outside formal credit facilities, citing the SME Foundation. This suggests an informative editorial lane around financial literacy, governance, records, risk, cash flow and financing readiness. It does not justify promises of funding or investment returns. Source: https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/77-cmsme-entrepreneurs-lack-access-bank-loans-sme-foundation-1505716

### Lifestyle and consumer shifts

The Business Standard's August 2026 interview on personal care described a shift among some urban and younger consumers toward skincare-led self-care, while traditional bar soap still dominated the category. This illustrates a wider editorial principle: premiumisation and lifestyle trends are segment-specific and must be supported with category evidence rather than assumed across all consumers. Source: https://www.tbsnews.net/supplement/rewriting-soap-opera-bar-bodywash-social-first-world-1529131

## 3. Division market maps and genre portfolios

## A. Arefin Legal & Corporate

**Position:** A credible public legal-education and corporate-law knowledge publication. Not a lead-generation or solicitation channel.

**Audience hypotheses to validate:** business owners and directors; professionals and compliance teams; students and researchers; members of the public seeking reliable explanations of legal processes.

**Market watch:** new statutes, amendments, rules, gazettes, Supreme Court judgments, High Court and Appellate Division developments, Bangladesh Bar Council notices, regulatory circulars, company and tax compliance changes, public legal questions and misinformation themes.

**Genres and formats:**
1. Judgment brief — carousel, long-form note, short explainer video.
2. Statute/section explainer — annotated graphic, FAQ, short video.
3. Legal myth vs law — carefully qualified carousel.
4. Procedure map — flowchart or checklist, with jurisdiction and date.
5. Legislative/regulatory update — dated bulletin with source link.
6. Corporate governance note — board duties, records, compliance concepts.
7. Legal history / landmark case — documentary explainer.
8. Research note — issue, authorities, reasoning, limitations.
9. Public-interest Q&A — general information only.
10. Glossary — Bangla/English legal terminology.

**Research rule:** primary authority first. Record case citation, court, date, bench if relevant, procedural posture, exact proposition, subsequent treatment and current status. Separate holding from obiter, legal rule from commentary, and general information from advice.

**Risk controls:** Bangladesh Bar Council Canons prohibit solicitation of professional employment by advertisement or other means. Avoid inducements, outcome guarantees, client acquisition CTAs and confidential case material. Review the current rules before publication. Source: https://www.barcouncil.gov.bd/disciplinary-jurisdiction/conduct-of-ethics-of-lawyers/

## B. Imran Arefin — Personal / Founder Publication

**Position:** First-person professional perspective, advocacy craft, leadership and public-interest thinking. Do not invent personal achievements, experiences or credentials.

**Audience hypotheses:** legal professionals, founders, business leaders, students, policy-aware audiences and people interested in disciplined professional development.

**Market watch:** legal-sector developments, business and institutional leadership, Bangladesh economic and policy debate, professional education, entrepreneurship, responsible AI and technology.

**Genres and formats:**
1. Founder field notes — concise first-person observations grounded in real work.
2. Weekly learning brief — one idea, evidence and practical implication.
3. Advocacy craft — preparation, research discipline, oral/written advocacy principles.
4. Leadership essay — decisions, trade-offs and accountability.
5. Book/report response — sourced reading notes, not fabricated endorsements.
6. Behind-the-work documentary — genuine process footage without confidential material.
7. Public-interest commentary — evidence-led, fair treatment of competing views.
8. Interview / conversation — only with actual consent and accurate attribution.
9. Visual quote card — only for verified quotations or clearly labelled original reflections.
10. Long-form video / podcast — substantive conversations and case-neutral professional learning.

## C. Arefin Avenue

**Position:** A Bangladesh-focused culture, place and experience publication with documentary integrity.

**Audience hypotheses:** domestic travellers, diaspora audiences, culture and heritage enthusiasts, young explorers, families and curious international viewers.

**Market watch:** domestic travel search interest, seasonal and weather conditions, access and transport updates, heritage conservation, local festivals, regional food, community enterprises, responsible tourism, safety notices and destination misinformation.

**Genres and formats:**
1. Destination field guide — practical, dated, map-aware carousel/article.
2. Mini-documentary — place, people, context and visual storytelling.
3. Local voices — consent-based interviews with residents, makers and guides.
4. Heritage explainer — history checked against credible records.
5. Food and craft story — origin, process, people and local context.
6. Solo / slow travel diary — authentic first-hand experience only.
7. Itinerary comparison — time, cost assumptions, access and limitations.
8. Seasonal travel brief — weather, closures, transport and safety verification.
9. Photo essay — captioned documentary images.
10. Responsible travel guide — environmental and community considerations.

**Research rule:** verify opening status, transport, access, prices, permits and safety close to publication. Mark first-hand observation separately from sourced information. Secure image/interview consent.

## D. IA² Holdings

**Position:** Group governance, strategy, investment literacy and enterprise operating knowledge. No unsupported investment claims or fabricated portfolio performance.

**Audience hypotheses:** SME owners, operators, professionals, prospective partners, entrepreneurs and financially literate readers.

**Market watch:** Bangladesh macroeconomic data, SME finance, banking and credit, tax/regulatory changes, capital markets, corporate governance, sector performance, productivity, trade and technology adoption.

**Genres and formats:**
1. Boardroom brief — one issue, evidence, decision implications.
2. SME operating playbook — governance, records, cash flow and controls.
3. Market pulse — dated data snapshot with source and caveat.
4. Business model teardown — public information only; distinguish analysis from fact.
5. Capital discipline series — budgeting, runway, risk and capital allocation.
6. Governance explainer — decision rights, reporting and accountability.
7. Sector map — value chain, competition and structural forces.
8. Founder/operator interview — consent and verified attribution.
9. Scenario analysis — assumptions and sensitivity, not predictions.
10. Annual/quarterly outlook — transparent methodology and uncertainty.

## E. C3 Lifestyle

**Position:** Curated lifestyle discovery and practical living editorial. Premium through usefulness, taste and credible curation—not unsupported wellness claims.

**Audience hypotheses:** urban professionals, design-conscious consumers, young households and people seeking considered everyday choices. Validate by category and platform.

**Market watch:** home and personal-care categories, design and interiors, consumer behaviour, wellness claims, local retail, price/value perceptions, seasonal routines and responsible consumption.

**Genres and formats:**
1. Curated edit — selected objects or ideas with transparent selection criteria.
2. How-it-works explainer — materials, features and care.
3. Everyday ritual — practical routine, no medical promises.
4. Design note — colour, space, materials and function.
5. Comparison guide — criteria, price/date and limitations.
6. Local maker story — real maker, consent and provenance.
7. Seasonal living guide — climate-appropriate, locally relevant advice.
8. Consumer myth check — evidence-based, non-medical where appropriate.
9. Editorial moodboard — palette, references and original composition.
10. Product review — only when genuinely tested or clearly labelled desk research.

## F. Bold Cotton

**Position:** A product-credible cotton and apparel brand with tactile visual storytelling and transparent product education.

**Audience hypotheses:** local apparel shoppers, style-conscious young adults, quality-focused basics buyers, gift buyers and wholesale/B2B prospects if that channel is confirmed.

**Market watch:** local fashion purchase patterns, cotton and blended-fibre preferences, garment construction, fit, climate comfort, pricing, competitor assortment, seasonal collections, care and sustainability claims.

**Genres and formats:**
1. Fabric school — fibre, yarn, weave, knit, GSM and finish.
2. Texture macro — close-up footage with accurate material labels.
3. Fit and silhouette guide — real garment measurements and model context.
4. Styling edit — multiple outfits, occasion and climate.
5. Care guide — washing, drying, shrinkage and colour care based on actual composition.
6. Product detail film — stitching, seams, collar, cuffs and finishing.
7. Collection story — real design rationale and product facts.
8. Behind the garment — genuine production/process evidence.
9. Comparison explainer — cotton vs blends, with balanced trade-offs.
10. Customer FAQ — factual sizing, availability, return and delivery policies.
11. Seasonal capsule — coordinated products and colour story.
12. Responsible-material note — claims supported by supplier documentation or certification.

**Research rule:** never invent fibre composition, GSM, origin, certification, shrinkage, durability or sustainability attributes. Confirm from product labels, supplier documentation or testing.

## 4. Content format portfolio

Every brand should rotate across formats to keep the feed lively while maintaining editorial quality:

| Format | Best use | Typical output |
|---|---|---|
| Short vertical video | Discovery, demonstrations, human presence | 20–60 second Reel/Short |
| Documentary short | Place, process, people, craft | 1–4 minute video |
| Carousel | Explain, compare, teach, save | 5–9 slides |
| Long-form video | Deep explanation, interview, story | 6–20+ minutes |
| Article / newsletter | Analysis, research and durable reference | 600–1,500 words as appropriate |
| Infographic | Data, process, timeline, comparison | One clear visual |
| Photo essay | Visual narrative and atmosphere | 5–10 curated images |
| Q&A / FAQ | Recurring audience questions | Short post, story or video |
| Poll / question prompt | Discover audience questions | Stories/community post |
| Case / field note | Real evidence and practical learning | Article, carousel or video |
| Live / recorded conversation | Expertise and community | Only when host, guest and topic are ready |
| Product demonstration | Real product detail and utility | Video, photo sequence or comparison |

Do not force every format onto every division. Format selection follows the audience need and the evidence available.

## 5. Platform roles

- **Facebook:** broad local distribution, community context, shareable explainers, video and discussion. Measure meaningful shares, comments, reach and referral actions.
- **Instagram:** visual identity, Reels, carousels, photography, product/brand storytelling and discovery. Use the account's Professional Dashboard Best Practices and account-specific insights; do not assume universal posting-frequency or algorithm rules.
- **YouTube:** Shorts for discovery and concise explainers; long-form for depth, documentary, interviews and durable search-led content. Evaluate viewer satisfaction, retention, returning viewers and topic fit, not views alone.
- **LinkedIn:** professional insight, corporate governance, leadership, research briefs and useful frameworks. Prioritise original evidence and distinctive human judgment over generic AI summaries.

## 6. Market and competitor intelligence workflow

For each brand, maintain a watchlist of:
- 8–12 direct or adjacent competitors/publications, selected after manual verification;
- 5–10 authoritative industry, regulator, government, research or trade sources;
- 10–20 audience questions and recurring search themes;
- relevant seasonal, cultural and commercial calendar events;
- platform-native format and creative trends;
- competitor positioning, content mix, visual identity and public engagement patterns.

For every signal, record:
1. Observation and date found.
2. Source and URL.
3. Market/segment and geography.
4. Evidence type: official data, survey, trade reporting, platform trend, competitor observation or audience feedback.
5. Confidence: high, medium or low.
6. What it might mean for the brand.
7. Proposed content test.
8. Result after publication.

Do not copy competitors. Identify unmet questions, weak explanations, underserved formats and distinctive perspectives.

## 7. Monthly content mix: starting experiment

The following is a test allocation, not a proven optimum. Adjust after at least 4–6 weeks of comparable platform data.

- 30% educational / useful explainers.
- 20% original analysis / thought leadership.
- 20% documentary / process / human stories.
- 15% practical guides / comparisons / demonstrations.
- 10% community questions / Q&A / interaction.
- 5% brand or product announcements, where applicable and compliant.

Legal and corporate channels should place more weight on evidence-led education and analysis. Arefin Avenue should place more weight on documentary storytelling. Bold Cotton and C3 may use more product, comparison and demonstration formats, with truthful commercial disclosures.

## 8. Trend validation and editorial gates

A trend is not automatically a content opportunity. Before adopting it, assess:
- audience relevance to the brand;
- Bangladesh/local market relevance;
- source quality and date;
- whether the trend is sustained or only a short spike;
- whether the brand has a credible point of view;
- legal, ethical, safety, rights and reputation risks;
- whether a useful original treatment is possible.

Use a three-level label:
- **Verified trend:** supported by credible current evidence and/or first-party account data.
- **Emerging signal:** early evidence; test cautiously.
- **Creative hypothesis:** an idea to experiment with, not a factual market claim.

Never describe an unverified trend as a market fact.

## 9. Experiment and measurement loop

Each month, run a small number of controlled creative tests:
- hook or opening style;
- short vs medium video length;
- carousel vs video for the same topic;
- Bangla vs English vs bilingual presentation;
- presenter-led vs voiceover vs text-led format;
- visual palette or cover treatment;
- educational vs narrative framing.

Change one major variable at a time where practical. Compare like-for-like content, audience, platform and time windows. Record reach, impressions, watch time, retention, completion, saves, shares, comments, profile actions, qualified enquiries where appropriate, and sales/revenue only where attribution is reliable. Do not equate correlation with causation.

## 10. Language and localisation

Choose Bangla, English or bilingual presentation by audience and subject:
- Legal explainers: precise Bangla with English legal terms where needed; include the source language/citation.
- Corporate/IA²: bilingual executive communication when it improves accessibility.
- Arefin Avenue: natural Bangla-first storytelling where appropriate, with English subtitles for wider discovery.
- C3 and Bold Cotton: language based on customer questions and conversion context; test rather than assume.
- Personal profile: preserve the author's real voice; avoid synthetic personal anecdotes.

## 11. Research record and production package

Every proposed content item should contain:
- brand and audience;
- audience question or need;
- genre and format;
- editorial angle and key takeaway;
- primary and secondary sources with dates and URLs;
- factual claims and the evidence supporting each;
- confidence and caveats;
- visual direction and palette;
- platform adaptations;
- rights/consent status;
- reviewer and approval state;
- post-publication metrics and learning.

## 12. First research sprints

1. **Legal:** build a dated tracker of official laws, rules, judgments and regulator notices; prepare a 10-topic public legal literacy backlog.
2. **Imran Arefin:** establish authentic founder themes and a bank of first-person notes based only on confirmed experience and supplied material.
3. **Arefin Avenue:** shortlist destinations and cultural subjects; validate seasonality, access, safety and local voices before filming.
4. **IA²:** build Bangladesh SME and corporate-governance topic map using Bangladesh Bank, BBS, SME Foundation, RJSC, NBR, BSEC and other primary sources as applicable.
5. **C3:** select a narrow initial category; research actual local competitors, price bands, customer questions and product evidence before broad lifestyle coverage.
6. **Bold Cotton:** verify the product range, fabric specifications, price points, sizing and fulfilment; benchmark local apparel brands and map customer objections before making claims.

## 13. Source hierarchy

1. Laws, judgments, gazettes, regulator and government datasets; company filings and product/supplier documentation.
2. Peer-reviewed research, recognised multilateral bodies, credible industry associations and audited reports.
3. Established business and trade journalism with named sources and dates.
4. Platform first-party creator resources and account analytics.
5. Competitor content and community discussion as qualitative signals only.

Search snippets, influencer claims, AI summaries and unattributed charts are leads, not final evidence. Open and inspect material sources before publishing important claims.
