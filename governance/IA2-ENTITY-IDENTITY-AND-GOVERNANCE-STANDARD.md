# IA² Entity Identity & Governance Standard

**Status:** Approved operating requirement; implementation draft  
**Owner:** IA² Holdings  
**Applies to:** Every current and future entity, product brand, platform, service line and public digital property established under the IMRAN AI / IA² ecosystem.

## 1. Mandatory rule

No entity may make a public launch, accept public customers, publish a commercial website, open a branded social channel, or sell under its name until its identity pack and applicable internal governance pack have been prepared, reviewed and approved. A prototype or private working preview may be used for development, but must be clearly labelled as such.

The founder retains responsibility for real-world legal compliance, execution of agreements, signatures, registrations, tax and regulatory decisions, financial transactions and negotiations. AI may research, draft, organise and flag issues; it must not represent that a legal document is executed, registered, compliant or legally effective without verified human action.

## 2. Required identity pack — one pack per entity

1. Official name and approved short name.
2. Entity purpose, positioning and intended audience.
3. Mission statement and vision statement.
4. Motto / tagline and approved short description.
5. Primary logo, compact mark, monochrome version, favicon / app icon and clear-space / minimum-size rules.
6. Logo rationale, permitted variants and prohibited alterations.
7. Colour system: primary, secondary, neutral, semantic colours and accessible contrast guidance, with HEX / RGB values.
8. Typography system, hierarchy and fallback fonts.
9. Brand voice, tone, vocabulary, writing rules and sample copy.
10. Visual direction for photography, illustration, iconography, layouts and motion.
11. Website, app, social, document, presentation and email signature usage examples.
12. Public claims register: credentials, service scope, geographic reach, licences, partnerships, client results and other claims requiring evidence.
13. Brand asset register, source files, version, owner and approval date.

Logos and brand assets are concepts until reviewed and approved. Check name conflicts, domain availability and trademark registers before adoption. Do not imply that a name or mark is legally cleared without a proper search and human review.

## 3. Required internal governance pack — tailored to legal form

Prepare only documents appropriate to the actual legal structure and jurisdiction. The pack may include:

- Entity charter / purpose and scope statement.
- Founder, promoter, member, shareholder or partner roles and authority matrix.
- Internal coordination, conduct, confidentiality and conflict-of-interest agreement.
- Inter-entity MOU / services agreement where entities share people, IP, systems, data, costs or customers.
- IP ownership, licensing, assignment and permitted-use schedule.
- Data protection, confidentiality, retention and access-control policy.
- Client / customer engagement terms and service-delivery acceptance criteria.
- Financial authority, expense approval, revenue allocation and recordkeeping policy.
- Risk register, complaints and incident escalation procedure.
- Board / management resolutions and approval records, where applicable.
- Constitutional documents, including Memorandum and Articles of Association or equivalent, only where required by the entity's chosen legal form and jurisdiction.

An AOA is not a universal document for every brand or project. A brand, product, internal division or unincorporated project should not be described as a separate company unless it has actually been constituted as one.

## 5. Approval workflow

1. Create an entity record and confirm whether it is a company, partnership, professional practice, division, product or project.
2. Prepare identity brief and competitive / name-conflict checks.
3. Produce logo and brand-system concepts; document source files and variants.
4. Draft the governance pack based on confirmed legal form, jurisdiction, activities and data flows.
5. Run professional, legal, regulatory, privacy, IP and accessibility review as relevant.
6. Founder approves public claims, brand assets, commercial terms and legal documents.
7. Record version, approval, effective date, responsible owner and review date.
8. Only then authorise public launch.

## 6. Document control

Every controlled document must show: document ID, entity, version, status (draft / review / approved / executed), owner, author, approval authority, creation date, last review date and next review trigger. Drafts must never be represented as signed or executed documents.

## 7. Non-negotiable safeguards

- No fabricated credentials, client testimonials, case results, licences, partnerships or performance claims.
- No legal advice or professional service beyond verified scope and appropriate human review.
- No unauthorised use of client data, confidential information, third-party marks or copyrighted assets.
- No public launch while required approvals or critical compliance checks remain outstanding.
- Keep entity brands distinct while maintaining a coherent parent-group design system.
